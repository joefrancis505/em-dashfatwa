When writing or reviewing prose, do not default to em dashes. Em dashes are
overused by LLMs as a crutch. Before using an em dash, consider whether the
sentence is better served by:

- A comma (for most parenthetical asides)
- Parentheses (for supplementary information)
- A colon (for amplification or explanation)
- A semicolon (for joining related independent clauses)
- Rewriting as two sentences

Reserve em dashes for situations where they are genuinely the best choice:
- Abrupt breaks in thought or sentence structure
- Interruptions in dialogue
- Setting off a list within a sentence that already uses commas
- A dramatic or emphatic shift that other punctuation cannot convey

If a passage contains more than one em dash (or pair), reread it and ask
whether each one earns its place.

This guidance applies when writing prose and when reviewing others' prose.
It does not apply to chat, code, commit messages, or other non-prose output.
